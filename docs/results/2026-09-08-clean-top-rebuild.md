## BRAM-capacity tradeoff probe

Leaving seven 64-bit × 512-word scratch buffers out of BRAM reduces the
physical count from 962 to exactly 955 RAMB36E1s, matching the device limit.
However, Yosys lowers those buffers to FF/mux logic, producing approximately
1.62 million AND gates; ABC9 cannot complete within the bounded host-memory
run. The capacity tradeoff therefore fits BRAM numerically but is not a viable
implementation strategy without a LUTRAM or state-partitioning representation.

Removing the BRAM hint only for those seven buffers and mapping them with
Yosys's XC7 LUTRAM descriptor yields 955 RAMB36E1 primitives plus 1,232
RAM64M blocks, with ABC9 completing on the resulting netlist. The remaining
implementation gap is arithmetic legalization: targeted carry mapping leaves
3,024 full-adder cells, while bit-level expansion again exceeds a million
cells. Thus LUTRAM solves the BRAM-capacity count but not yet the complete
placement path.

The remaining `$fa` cells are not scalar bookkeeping: each sampled cell has
`WIDTH=128` and exposes 128-bit `A`, `B`, and `C` operands. The 3,024 cells
therefore represent 387,072 full-adder bits, or about 96,768 CARRY4 slices if
implemented directly (four bits per slice). Expanding them to gates produced
1,941,504 cells (774,144 AND, 387,072 OR, and 777,216 XOR), confirming that
the current generated arithmetic is beyond a practical Kintex-7 placement
before routing. This is a measured generated-design capacity blocker, not a
missing PCIe/JTAG or DDR3 integration setting.

The widening source is identified in the generated helper: the Futil uses
`std_smult_pipe(64)`, but its signed multiply implementation concatenates a
second `WIDTH`-bit sign extension before multiplying. Yosys consequently sees
24 unsigned 128×128 multipliers (the 24 product sites include the final
normalization and logits paths), then materializes their accumulators as the
3,024 128-bit `$fa` cells. This gives a concrete compiler/runtime repair
target: preserve the 64-bit fixed-point result contract while avoiding the
unnecessary 128-bit operand widening. The repair now computes unsigned
`WIDTH`-bit magnitudes, multiplies those at `WIDTH × WIDTH`, and applies the
two's-complement sign correction into the existing `2*WIDTH` result register.
On the actual generated helper, Yosys reports `A_WIDTH=64`, `B_WIDTH=64`, and
`Y_WIDTH=128` for the signed specialization.

However, full-top elaboration through the production Yosys-Slang frontend
reconstructs 128-bit operands from the 128-bit assignment context. The hook is
therefore opt-in and is not yet a production optimization; a compiler/runtime
change must force the partial-product decomposition (or an equivalent DSP
primitive boundary) before this hypothesis can reduce the placed design.
