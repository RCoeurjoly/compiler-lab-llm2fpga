# Stage DDR3-backed RC before model scaling

The learned-tensor port classification and logical address map are derived from `memory-abi.json` and `calyx_memory_bindings` receipts and recorded in a deterministic manifest; they are not hand-maintained.

The verification and optimization program begins two host-only tracks in parallel: establish the direct raw-SV closure needed to simulate generated RC RTL, and integrate every learned tensor (embeddings, weights, biases, and positional tables) with DDR3 through UberDDR3 RTL and the manufacturer's memory model. The integration inserts a generated or parameterized ABI-preserving adapter that routes only those immutable learned-tensor ports onto UberDDR3's bounded user port, rather than changing the compiler's memory ABI. Token input, mutable activations/scratch, output buffers, and scalar arithmetic, quantization, and mask constants remain local in this first DDR3 stage. The first end-to-end milestone is one exact DDR3-backed RC result against frozen PyTorch, followed by its selected-input campaign. The first DDR3 transport retains the frozen tensor image's logical addresses and byte layout verbatim; packing, caching, and address remapping are deferred. Only then do we make a bounded TinyStories-1M attempt through the same lowering and SV pipeline, synthesizing the accepted DDR3-backed top with its bounded UberDDR3 interface rather than the historical many-port compute shell, and obtain a Yosys/nextpnr-xilinx resource-utilization report. A scaling estimate is the fallback only if that direct probe reaches a recorded resource limit; after the result, we choose deliberately between scaling the design and further RC optimization.

This order makes the memory-interface and memory-model contract observable before model size obscures failures or cost attribution, without requiring a physical board. UberDDR3 RTL, the board wrapper, and the manufacturer model are pinned by revision or content hash rather than read from ambient checkouts. An automated compatibility audit proves the RC external-memory ABI and UberDDR3 user-port width, ordering, and outstanding-request assumptions before the adapter arbitration design is selected. A deterministic UberDDR3 transport contract test then validates exact data, address translation, and completion ordering against the manufacturer model before full RC integration. The manufacturer model remains simulation-only; synthesis and P&R include synthesizable UberDDR3, the accelerator, and the real DDR3 pin and clock constraints of the YPCB-00338-1P1 XC7K480T board rather than a P&R-only probe XDC. The scale probe has a 26 GiB per-process memory cap, a four-hour cap per stage, a twelve-hour total cap, and records its last completed artifact, tool provenance, and resource use when it reaches a frontier. The scaling estimate must state its assumptions and is not a functional-equivalence claim. Exhaustive Verilator simulation is not a prerequisite; formal or compositional proof remains a later separately scoped track that needs an extractable formal specification of the frozen PyTorch semantics. This decision supersedes earlier plans that defer DDR3 until a physical-board RC checkpoint, for the host-only pipeline and simulation scope.
